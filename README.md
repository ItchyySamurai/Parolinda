# Parolinda

A Ruzzle-style Italian word game, made for Linda. Solo play, fully offline once
installed, no ads, no accounts, no network calls at any point.

*Parole* + *Linda* — and it reads as *parola linda*, since **lindo/linda** is
Italian for neat, tidy, spotless.

The repo root **is** the site — that's what lets GitHub Pages serve it with a
single dropdown, no build step and no path juggling.

```
index.html  style.css  game.js  dawg.js  dict.bin
progress.js    levels, trophies, the day diary
collection.js  themes, titles, avatars, the pass track
manifest.webmanifest  sw.js
icons/      the PWA icons, L monogram drawn procedurally
build/      how dict.bin and the icons were made; not needed to run the game
```

## Making it hers

Two constants at the top of `game.js`:

```js
var PLAYER = 'Linda';
var DEDICA = 'Fatto con affetto per la mamma.';
```

`PLAYER` drives the record label and every line of praise. `DEDICA` is the line
at the bottom of *Come si gioca* — **put your name in it**; it currently doesn't
say who it's from.

The home screen greeting rotates on every visit to the menu and follows the
clock: *Buongiorno* before noon, *Buonasera* after six, plus a general pool
(*Facciamo due parole?*, *Quante ne troviamo oggi?*). Edit `greetingPool()` in
`game.js` to add more. Its adjectives agree with a feminine subject, as do the
level names in `progress.js`.

The palette lives as CSS variables at the top of `style.css` — `--accent` is
the green, and the background carries the same hue so the green reads as the
room's colour rather than a highlight on a colder one. Every pair clears WCAG
AA. The icon is regenerated by `python build/make_icons.py`, which keeps its
own copy of the three main colours.

## Putting it on her phone

1. Create an empty GitHub repo (public — Pages needs that on a free account).
2. From this folder:

```bash
git init && git add . && git commit -m "Parolinda" && git branch -M main
```

3. Point it at your repo and push:

```bash
git remote add origin https://github.com/USERNAME/REPO.git && git push -u origin main
```

4. On GitHub: **Settings → Pages → Source: Deploy from a branch → main / (root)**.
   Wait a minute, then the site is at `https://USERNAME.github.io/REPO/`.
5. On her phone, open that link **in Chrome**, then menu **⋮ → Aggiungi a
   schermata Home** (Add to Home screen). Chrome may offer "Installa app"
   instead — either is fine.

That's the only moment she needs internet. The service worker precaches
everything including the dictionary, so from then on it launches from the
home-screen icon and runs with the phone in airplane mode.

Serving it over plain `http://` from a LAN address works for a quick look, but
Chrome will not register a service worker or offer to install outside a secure
context — so the home-screen icon and the offline behaviour only appear once
it's on HTTPS.

To ship a fix later: edit, **bump `CACHE` in `sw.js`**, push. Her copy updates
the next time she opens it while online — she has to close and reopen once,
because the running page keeps the old code until it is reloaded. If you don't
bump `CACHE`, the old files keep being served and nothing appears to change.

## Modes

**Sfida del giorno** — one board per calendar day, the same for everyone,
seeded from the date with mulberry32: letters and bonus tiles alike. She can
replay it as often as she likes and only her best score for the day is kept.

It's fixed at **3 minutes** on purpose. A daily board is only worth comparing
if the clock is the same, both against yesterday and against whoever else is
playing it. Two people on separate phones with no connection between them get
identical boards and comparable scores.

**Partita libera** — a fresh random board, with its own record per round length
(2, 3 or 5 minutes), kept separately from the daily.

**Senza fretta** — no clock at all. The timer counts up instead of down and the
round ends when she taps *Ho finito*. It sets no record, since with unlimited
time there is nothing to compare, but the words, points and trophies all count
normally. The score trophies are excluded there for the same reason — see
`timed` in `progress.js`.

## Livelli, trofei e diario

All of this lives in `progress.js`, and it is built on one rule: **everything
only ever goes up.** Nothing expires, nothing is lost by not playing for a
week, and there is deliberately **no consecutive-day streak** — the diary
counts total days played instead. A streak is the most motivating mechanic
available and also the most anxiety-producing, and the whole appeal of this app
is that it asks nothing of her.

**Livelli** run from *Curiosa* to *Regina delle parole* across **30 named
levels** on cumulative points. The ladder is long on purpose: level 2 after a
couple of games, level 10 after a few weeks, level 30 after well over a year of
steady play. She plays a lot, so the ceiling should stay out of reach.

Everyone starts at level 1 with zero points, including a player who had the
older version — that was a deliberate call, so the climb is introduced from its
beginning rather than dropping someone into the middle of a ladder they never
saw. Games, words and best word carry over untouched; only the points counter
starts fresh.

**Trofei** are 13 one-time, permanent awards — a first word, an 8- and a
10-letter word, 20 and 30 words in a round, three score thresholds, beating her
own daily result, finishing a Senza fretta round, playing on 7 and on 30
different days, and 1000 words in total. Locked ones stay visible at low
opacity so they read as goals.

**Il mio diario** is reached from the level strip on the home screen or from
the results screen. It holds the level bar, lifetime totals, the trophy case,
and a month calendar: days she played are shaded, days she played the daily
challenge show the score, and the months page backwards but not into the
future. Weeks start on Monday.

## I percorsi

`collection.js` holds **twelve passes in a fixed sequence**. The first is open
from the start and each one unlocks the next when it is finished. Only one is
active at a time and she chooses which, from the list on the pass screen;
points from a round go to whichever pass she has picked. Finishing one moves
her on automatically, so points are never banked into something already full.

Each pass has **five traguardi** paying out, in order: a title, an avatar
symbol, a second title, a second symbol, and finally the pass's own theme. Tier
costs scale by 25% per pass, so *Il giardino* is about a week of play and
*L'oro* is a real undertaking. Finishing all twelve costs roughly 185,000
points — a few months of steady play, and still well short of level 30.

**There is no calendar anywhere in this file.** A pass cannot run out, cannot
be missed, and waits exactly where she left it. That is the point: the shape of
a pass — a track, tiers, rewards to climb towards — with none of the parts that
exist to make someone feel late. It replaced an earlier seasonal version for
the same reason.

## La collezione

14 themes, 34 titles and 33 avatar symbols — 81 things to collect — unlocked
by level, by trophy, or from the pass track, and equipped by tapping. Locked
ones stay visible with the requirement written underneath.

Themes work by redefining the same CSS custom properties on
`:root[data-theme="..."]`, so the entire interface follows without any component
knowing a theme exists. **Every theme was checked pair-by-pair against WCAG AA**
— text, muted text, accents, button ink, tile letters and both bonus colours —
and the worst pair across all fourteen sits at 5.19:1. If you add one, run that
check again; the two bonus colours on the tile are what fails first, because the
tile is light in every theme while the bonus text is dark.

## First run and updates

`INTRO_SLIDES` in `game.js` is a five-card tutorial shown the first time the app
is ever opened; `NEWS_SLIDES` is a what's-new deck shown once to someone who
already had an older version. Which one appears is decided by `maybeIntro()`
from `seenVersion` against `APP_VERSION` — **bump `APP_VERSION` and rewrite
`NEWS_SLIDES` when you ship something worth announcing.** The tutorial is always
reachable again from *Come si gioca* on the home screen.

## Accessibility

**Dimensione del testo** on the home screen has three steps. One `--ts`
multiplier in `style.css` scales every piece of reading text. The board is
deliberately excluded — its letters are sized to the tile and already large,
and scaling them would only push them out of their squares.

## Progress, and moving it between devices

Progress lives in `localStorage` under the `parolinda.` prefix: a record per
round length, a best score per daily board, lifetime stats, trophies, and the
set of days played. The app asks for **persistent storage** on load and again
on the first tap, so Android doesn't evict it when the device runs low — Chrome
grants this silently to an installed PWA.

`localStorage` is per-device, so **her phone and tablet keep separate scores**,
and nothing syncs them. Chrome's account sync does not cover it. The daily
board itself is identical on both, since it comes from the date rather than
from storage.

*Backup dei progressi* on the home screen closes the gap without a server. It
encodes everything into a string she can copy or send to herself, and the other
device pastes it back. Restoring **merges by keeping the higher score**, never
overwriting, so it is safe on a device that already has progress and doing it
twice changes nothing. Trophies merge as a union with the earlier award date
winning. Malformed codes are rejected before anything is written.

The code grows by roughly 30 bytes for every day she plays, so it is a few
hundred characters now and a few thousand after a year — still fine to paste or
send, but it is not going to stay short forever.

## The dictionary

`dict.bin` is 2,671,495 Italian word forms in 558 KB.

The source is the LibreOffice/Hunspell `it_IT` dictionary (GPL-3), which stores
**stems plus affix rules** rather than finished words. `build/build_dict.py`
expands those rules into every inflected form — this is the step that decides
whether the game feels right, because shipping the stems raw would reject
almost every conjugated verb and plural.

It then:

- folds accents to base letters, so *città* is found by tracing `CITTA`;
- drops words needing J K W X Y, which are not Italian letters and never appear
  on the board;
- drops capitalised entries (proper nouns, acronyms);
- drops roman numerals — the source lists them lowercase, so `clv` and `mcv`
  would otherwise be playable words. The script prints everything it removes
  this way, so a real word going missing is visible rather than silent;
- sets a **3-letter minimum**. At 2 letters the source's unit symbols (`mq`,
  `cl`, `hg`, `dm`) dominate, and they're farmable by tapping adjacent pairs at
  random. Change `MIN_LEN` in `build/build_dict.py` if you disagree.

The result is packed into a DAWG: a deduplicated prefix graph, one uint32 per
edge, walked directly out of the `ArrayBuffer` so an old tablet never holds
2.6 million JavaScript strings in memory.

To rebuild:

```bash
python build/build_dict.py
```

## Board and scoring

Letter values are the Italian Scrabble table. The **tile distribution is
measured from the dictionary**, counting each letter once per word across words
of 3–8 letters — the lengths a player actually finds. Deriving it from the whole
list instead would shape the board around 16-letter inflections nobody reaches.

Every board is solved before it is handed over, and redrawn unless it holds at
least 80 words, 4 of them 6+ letters, and 5–9 vowels. That costs about 1 ms.
A raw random board is often a dud; this is why Ruzzle boards feel generous.

Word score = (letter values × any letter multiplier) × word multipliers, plus a
length bonus of +5/+10/+15 at 5/6/7 letters and +20 at 8 or more.

## Notes

- Both input styles work: drag across the letters, or tap them one at a time
  and press **✓**. Dragging is not easy for everyone.
- Praise on the results screen reacts to the round — a new record, a long word,
  coming close to her best — rather than firing the same exclamation every time.
- The round pauses automatically if she switches away from the app.
- Sound is synthesised with WebAudio — there are no audio files to ship — and
  can be turned off on the home screen.
